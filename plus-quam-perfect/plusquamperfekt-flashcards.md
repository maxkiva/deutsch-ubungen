# 🇩🇪 German Plusquamperfekt – Flashcard Set

A detailed practice set covering the German **Plusquamperfekt** (past perfect) tense.  
Each card includes the rule, examples, and English commentary explaining *why* and *when* it works that way.

---

## 📌 WHAT IS THE PLUSQUAMPERFEKT?

The **Plusquamperfekt** (also called *Vorvergangenheit* — "pre-past") is the German equivalent of the English **past perfect** (*had done*, *had gone*, *had eaten*).

It describes an action that was **already completed before another past event** took place.

> **English parallel:**  
> *By the time she arrived, I **had already eaten**.*  
> *He **had lived** in Berlin before he moved to Munich.*

## 🟦 FORMATION

---

### Card F-1 — Building the Plusquamperfekt with *haben*

**Rule:** Most verbs form the Plusquamperfekt with the **Präteritum of *haben*** (*hatte/hattest/hatte/hatten/hattet/hatten*) + **Partizip II** of the main verb.

**Formula:**
> **hatte / hattest / hatte / hatten / hattet / hatten** + [Partizip II]

| Person | Präteritum of *haben* | Example (machen) |
|--------|-----------------------|------------------|
| ich | **hatte** | ich hatte gemacht |
| du | **hattest** | du hattest gemacht |
| er/sie/es | **hatte** | er hatte gemacht |
| wir | **hatten** | wir hatten gemacht |
| ihr | **hattet** | ihr hattet gemacht |
| sie/Sie | **hatten** | sie hatten gemacht |

**Example:**
> Ich **hatte** das Buch schon **gelesen**, bevor der Film herauskam.
> *I had already read the book before the film came out.*

💬 **Commentary:** Think of *hatte* + Partizip II as the past version of *hat* + Partizip II (Perfekt). You already know the Perfekt (*Ich habe gelesen*) — just swap *habe* → *hatte* and you have the Plusquamperfekt. The Partizip II never changes regardless of person or number.

---

### Card F-2 — Building the Plusquamperfekt with *sein*

**Rule:** Verbs that form the Perfekt with *sein* also use the **Präteritum of *sein*** (*war/warst/war/waren/wart/waren*) + **Partizip II** in the Plusquamperfekt.

**Formula:**
> **war / warst / war / waren / wart / waren** + [Partizip II]

| Person | Präteritum of *sein* | Example (gehen) |
|--------|----------------------|-----------------|
| ich | **war** | ich war gegangen |
| du | **warst** | du warst gegangen |
| er/sie/es | **war** | er war gegangen |
| wir | **waren** | wir waren gegangen |
| ihr | **wart** | ihr wart gegangen |
| sie/Sie | **waren** | sie waren gegangen |

**Example:**
> Als wir ankamen, **war** er schon nach Hause **gegangen**.
> *By the time we arrived, he had already gone home.*

💬 **Commentary:** The same verbs that use *sein* in the Perfekt (*Er ist gegangen*) use *war* in the Plusquamperfekt (*Er war gegangen*). The rule for choosing *haben* vs. *sein* does not change — only the helper verb shifts from present to past tense.

---

### Card F-3 — Which Verbs Take *sein*?

**Rule:** A verb uses *sein* (not *haben*) as its auxiliary when it meets **at least one** of these conditions:

1. **Motion from A to B** (change of location):
   > gehen, kommen, fahren, fliegen, laufen, reisen, schwimmen*, fallen, steigen …

2. **Change of state** (becoming something different):
   > werden, einschlafen, aufwachen, sterben, wachsen, einfrieren, schmelzen …

3. **A small fixed group** (must be memorized):
   > *sein* (to be), *bleiben* (to stay), *passieren* (to happen), *geschehen* (to happen), *gelingen* (to succeed), *misslingen* (to fail)

**Examples:**
> Er **war** nach Berlin **geflogen**. — *He had flown to Berlin.*
> Sie **war** plötzlich **eingeschlafen**. — *She had suddenly fallen asleep.*
> Es **war** etwas Seltsames **passiert**. — *Something strange had happened.*

⚠️ *schwimmen* uses *sein* when movement across a distance is implied (*Er ist über den See geschwommen*), but *haben* when the activity is the focus (*Er hat eine Stunde geschwommen*). Context matters!

💬 **Commentary:** When in doubt, ask yourself: *Does this verb describe moving somewhere or changing into a different state?* If yes → *sein*. Otherwise → *haben*. This rule applies identically to Perfekt and Plusquamperfekt.

## 🟥 PARTIZIP II (Past Participle)

---

### Card P-1 — Regular (Weak) Verbs

**Rule:** Regular verbs form the Partizip II with **ge- + stem + -(e)t**.

| Infinitive | Stem | Partizip II |
|------------|------|-------------|
| machen (to make/do) | mach | ge**mach**t |
| kaufen (to buy) | kauf | ge**kauf**t |
| spielen (to play) | spiel | ge**spiel**t |
| arbeiten (to work) | arbeit | ge**arbeit**et* |
| warten (to wait) | wart | ge**wart**et* |

> *Stems ending in **-t** or **-d** insert an extra **-e-** before the suffix for pronunciation: *gearbeitet*, *gewartet*.

**Examples in Plusquamperfekt:**
> Ich **hatte** drei Stunden lang **gearbeitet**, bevor ich eine Pause machte.
> *I had worked for three hours before I took a break.*

> Sie **hatte** ein neues Kleid **gekauft**, bevor die Party begann.
> *She had bought a new dress before the party started.*

💬 **Commentary:** The *ge-* prefix is the clearest marker that you're looking at a Partizip II. If you see *ge-…-t*, it's almost certainly a regular verb's past participle. Recognizing this pattern quickly helps you parse Plusquamperfekt sentences at speed.

---

### Card P-2 — Irregular (Strong) Verbs

**Rule:** Strong verbs form the Partizip II with **ge- + (changed) stem + -en**. The stem often changes vowel (ablaut). These must be learned individually.

| Infinitive | Partizip II | Meaning |
|------------|-------------|---------|
| gehen | ge**gang**en | gone |
| kommen | ge**komm**en | come |
| sehen | ge**seh**en | seen |
| essen | ge**gess**en | eaten |
| trinken | ge**trunk**en | drunk |
| schreiben | ge**schrieb**en | written |
| fahren | ge**fahr**en | driven/traveled |
| nehmen | ge**nomm**en | taken |
| lesen | ge**les**en | read |
| finden | ge**fund**en | found |
| sprechen | ge**sproch**en | spoken |
| geben | ge**geb**en | given |
| schlafen | ge**schlaf**en | slept |
| bleiben | ge**blieb**en | stayed |
| rufen | ge**ruf**en | called |

**Examples in Plusquamperfekt:**
> Er **hatte** nichts **gegessen**, als er ins Büro kam.
> *He had eaten nothing when he came into the office.*

> Wir **waren** den ganzen Weg zu Fuß **gegangen**.
> *We had walked the whole way.*

💬 **Commentary:** The vowel change in the stem (e.g., *schreiben → geschrieben*, *trinken → getrunken*) follows patterns called "ablaut series." Learning verbs in groups by vowel pattern (*ei→ie*, *i→u*, *e→o*) is far more efficient than memorizing each individually. The -en ending on the Partizip II of strong verbs distinguishes them from the -t ending of weak verbs.

---

### Card P-3 — Mixed (Irregular Weak) Verbs

**Rule:** A small group of verbs has a **changed stem** (like strong verbs) but the **-t ending** (like weak verbs). These are called *gemischte Verben* (mixed verbs) and must be memorized.

| Infinitive | Partizip II | Meaning |
|------------|-------------|---------|
| bringen | ge**brach**t | brought |
| denken | ge**dach**t | thought |
| kennen | ge**kann**t | known (a person/place) |
| nennen | ge**nann**t | named/called |
| rennen | ge**rann**t | run (sprinted) |
| wissen | ge**wuss**t | known (a fact) |
| brennen | ge**brann**t | burned |

**Examples in Plusquamperfekt:**
> Ich **hatte** nie **gedacht**, dass er lügen würde.
> *I had never thought that he would lie.*

> Sie **hatte** ihn schon lange **gekannt**, bevor sie heirateten.
> *She had known him for a long time before they got married.*

💬 **Commentary:** The trap here is that the stem change makes these look like strong verbs (*bringen → gebracht* mirrors *denken → gedacht*), but the -t ending gives them away as "weak-style." There are only about a dozen of these — learn them as a set and you've mastered all of them at once.

---

### Card P-4 — Verbs with Inseparable Prefixes (No *ge-*)

**Rule:** Verbs beginning with the **inseparable prefixes** *be-, er-, emp-, ent-, ge-, miss-, ver-, zer-* do **not** add *ge-* to their Partizip II.

| Infinitive | Partizip II | Meaning |
|------------|-------------|---------|
| besuchen | besucht | visited |
| erklären | erklärt | explained |
| empfehlen | empfohlen | recommended |
| entscheiden | entschieden | decided |
| vergessen | vergessen | forgotten |
| verstehen | verstanden | understood |
| zerstören | zerstört | destroyed |
| bezahlen | bezahlt | paid |

**Examples in Plusquamperfekt:**
> Er **hatte** seinen Schlüssel **vergessen**, deshalb kamen wir zu spät.
> *He had forgotten his key, so we were late.*

> Sie **hatten** die Rechnung schon **bezahlt**, als wir ankamen.
> *They had already paid the bill when we arrived.*

💬 **Commentary:** These prefixes are "glued on" — they never separate from the verb. Because the prefix already breaks up the word, adding *ge-* on top would make pronunciation awkward. The rule is absolute: inseparable prefix = no *ge-* in Partizip II. This applies to regular forms (-t) and irregular forms (-en) alike.

---

### Card P-5 — Verbs with Separable Prefixes (*ge-* in the Middle)

**Rule:** Verbs with **separable prefixes** (*ab-, an-, auf-, aus-, ein-, mit-, vor-, zu-*, etc.) insert *ge-* **between the prefix and the stem**.

| Infinitive | Partizip II | Meaning |
|------------|-------------|---------|
| aufmachen | auf**ge**macht | opened |
| anrufen | an**ge**rufen | called (phoned) |
| einkaufen | ein**ge**kauft | shopped |
| aufstehen | auf**ge**standen | gotten up |
| mitnehmen | mit**ge**nommen | taken along |
| zumachen | zu**ge**macht | closed |
| ankommen | an**ge**kommen | arrived |
| vorstellen | vor**ge**stellt | introduced/imagined |

**Examples in Plusquamperfekt:**
> Ich **war** um 6 Uhr **aufgestanden**.
> *I had gotten up at 6 o'clock.*

> Sie **hatte** schon **eingekauft**, bevor der Regen begann.
> *She had already done the shopping before the rain started.*

> Er **hatte** mich dreimal **angerufen**, ohne eine Antwort zu bekommen.
> *He had called me three times without getting an answer.*

💬 **Commentary:** The separable prefix stays attached to the front in the Partizip II — it does NOT separate here. The *ge-* is sandwiched in the middle: *ein + ge + kauft = eingekauft*. Spotting the *ge-* in the middle of a long word is a reliable signal that you're looking at a Partizip II of a separable verb.

---

### Card P-6 — Verbs Ending in *-ieren* (No *ge-*)

**Rule:** Verbs borrowed from foreign languages that end in **-ieren** form the Partizip II with **just -(ie)rt** — no *ge-* prefix.

| Infinitive | Partizip II | Meaning |
|------------|-------------|---------|
| studieren | studiert | studied |
| passieren | passiert | happened |
| fotografieren | fotografiert | photographed |
| telefonieren | telefoniert | phoned |
| reparieren | repariert | repaired |
| organisieren | organisiert | organized |
| reagieren | reagiert | reacted |
| funktionieren | funktioniert | worked/functioned |

**Examples in Plusquamperfekt:**
> Er **hatte** Medizin **studiert**, bevor er die Firma gründete.
> *He had studied medicine before he founded the company.*

> Das Gerät **hatte** nicht richtig **funktioniert**, also kauften wir ein neues.
> *The device had not worked properly, so we bought a new one.*

💬 **Commentary:** The *-ieren* ending is a strong marker of foreign-origin verbs (mostly from French, Latin, or English). These verbs are always weak (regular) and never take *ge-*. The ending *-iert* is easy to spot. Good news: there are hundreds of these verbs, and if you know the foreign-language root, you likely already know the German word.

## 🟨 WHEN TO USE PLUSQUAMPERFEKT

---

### Card U-1 — Sequence of Past Events (The Core Use)

**Rule:** Use the Plusquamperfekt to describe an action that happened **before** another past action. The more recent past event uses **Perfekt** or **Präteritum**; the earlier event uses **Plusquamperfekt**.

**Time line:**
```
EARLIER (Plusquamperfekt) ──────→ LATER (Präteritum/Perfekt) ──────→ NOW
       Er hatte gegessen                  Als er ankam
```

**Examples:**
> Als er nach Hause **kam**, **hatte** ich schon **gekocht**.
> *When he came home, I had already cooked.*

> Sie **rief** mich an, aber ich **hatte** mein Handy schon **ausgeschaltet**.
> *She called me, but I had already switched off my phone.*

> Wir **erkannten** das Gebäude sofort, weil wir Fotos **gesehen hatten**.
> *We recognized the building immediately because we had seen photos.*

💬 **Commentary:** The Plusquamperfekt does not express *how long ago* something happened — it expresses *that it happened before* some other past moment. If both actions happen at the same time in the past, you do NOT use Plusquamperfekt. The "before/after" relationship is the key trigger.

---

### Card U-2 — With *nachdem* (after)

**Rule:** The conjunction **nachdem** (after) almost always triggers the Plusquamperfekt in its clause, because it explicitly signals that one action was completed *before* the next began.

**Formula:**
> **Nachdem** + [subject + Plusquamperfekt], [main clause in Präteritum/Perfekt].

**Examples:**
> **Nachdem** sie das Buch **gelesen hatte**, schrieb sie eine Rezension.
> *After she had read the book, she wrote a review.*

> **Nachdem** wir **gegessen hatten**, machten wir einen Spaziergang.
> *After we had eaten, we went for a walk.*

> **Nachdem** er **angekommen war**, rief er sofort seine Familie an.
> *After he had arrived, he immediately called his family.*

⚠️ Note the **verb-final word order** in the *nachdem* clause (because it's a subordinate clause). The auxiliary (*hatte/war*) goes to the very end, after the Partizip II.

💬 **Commentary:** *Nachdem* is the single strongest indicator of Plusquamperfekt in German. Whenever you see *nachdem*, your brain should automatically switch to Plusquamperfekt for that clause. The pairing *nachdem + Plusquamperfekt / main clause + Präteritum* is a classic grammar test pattern.

---

### Card U-3 — With *als* (when) and *bevor* (before)

**Rule:** *Als* (when — single past event) and *bevor/ehe* (before) can also appear with Plusquamperfekt to emphasize the temporal sequence.

**With *als*:**
> **Als** ich **aufgewacht war**, schien die Sonne schon.
> *When I had woken up, the sun was already shining.*

> **Als** er das Ergebnis **gehört hatte**, wurde er sehr blass.
> *When he had heard the result, he turned very pale.*

**With *bevor/ehe*:**
> Sie verließ das Haus, **bevor** er **zurückgekehrt war**.
> *She left the house before he had returned.*

> Er hatte das Projekt abgeschlossen, **ehe** die Frist **abgelaufen war**.
> *He had completed the project before the deadline had expired.*

💬 **Commentary:** With *als*, the Plusquamperfekt emphasizes that the first action was fully completed before the second started. With *bevor/ehe*, the Plusquamperfekt in the *bevor* clause is especially common when you want to stress that something was (or was not) done in time. Compare: *Sie ging, bevor er kam* (simple sequence) vs. *Sie war gegangen, bevor er kam* (fully done and gone before he arrived).

---

### Card U-4 — *Kaum … als* and *Sobald* (Barely … when / As soon as)

**Rule:** The construction **kaum … als** (barely/scarcely … when) and **sobald** (as soon as) frequently pair with the Plusquamperfekt to emphasize immediacy.

**Examples with *kaum … als*:**
> **Kaum hatte** ich mich hingesetzt, **als** das Telefon klingelte.
> *Scarcely had I sat down when the phone rang.*

> **Kaum war** er **eingeschlafen**, **als** das Baby zu weinen begann.
> *He had barely fallen asleep when the baby began to cry.*

**Examples with *sobald*:**
> **Sobald** sie **angekommen war**, begann sie mit der Arbeit.
> *As soon as she had arrived, she started working.*

💬 **Commentary:** *Kaum … als* inverts the subject and verb in the first clause (*Kaum hatte ich* instead of *Ich hatte kaum*) — this is a literary/formal inversion typical of written German. The Plusquamperfekt here adds the nuance of "it had just barely happened." *Sobald* is more neutral and common in both spoken and written German.

---

### Card U-5 — Reported Speech and Narrative Distance

**Rule:** The Plusquamperfekt can mark past-before-past in **indirect speech** and in **narrative** contexts when background information is given.

**Narrative background:**
> Er trat ins Zimmer. Jemand **hatte** ein Fenster **eingeschlagen**. Das Licht **war** ausgeschaltet **worden**. Offensichtlich **war** hier etwas passiert.
> *He stepped into the room. Someone had broken a window. The light had been switched off. Obviously something had happened here.*

**Indirect speech:**
> Sie sagte, dass sie das Dokument bereits **abgeschickt hatte**.
> *She said that she had already sent the document.*

💬 **Commentary:** In literary German, the Plusquamperfekt is used frequently to create "layers" of past time — the story moves in Präteritum, while backstory or prior events are rendered in Plusquamperfekt. This layering gives readers a clear sense of which events came first without needing explicit time words.

## 🟩 MODAL VERBS IN PLUSQUAMPERFEKT

---

### Card M-1 — Modals with a Full Verb (Double Infinitive)

**Rule:** When a modal verb (*können, müssen, dürfen, wollen, sollen, mögen*) is used with a full verb in the Plusquamperfekt, the construction uses:

> **hatte** + [infinitive of main verb] + [infinitive of modal] *(double infinitive at the end)*

⚠️ The **Partizip II of the modal is NOT used** when a main verb is present — use the **infinitive** instead. This is called the *Ersatzinfinitiv* (substitute infinitive).

**Examples:**
> Ich **hatte** das nicht **tun wollen**.
> *I had not wanted to do that.*

> Er **hatte** früher **aufstehen müssen**.
> *He had had to get up earlier.*

> Wir **hatten** nicht **kommen können**.
> *We had not been able to come.*

💬 **Commentary:** The double infinitive at the end of the clause (*tun wollen*, *aufstehen müssen*) looks and sounds unusual at first. Remember: *wollen, müssen, können* etc. only show their Partizip II form (*gewollt, gemusst, gekonnt*) when used **alone** (without a second verb): *Ich hatte es gewollt* = I had wanted it. With a full verb, always use the infinitive form.

---

### Card M-2 — Modals Used Alone (Without a Main Verb)

**Rule:** When a modal verb stands **without** a main verb (the main verb is implied or omitted), it forms the Plusquamperfekt normally with **hatte + Partizip II of the modal**.

| Modal | Partizip II |
|-------|-------------|
| können | gekonnt |
| müssen | gemusst |
| dürfen | gedurft |
| wollen | gewollt |
| sollen | gesollt |
| mögen | gemocht |

**Examples:**
> Er **hatte** es nicht **gekonnt** — er war zu schwach. *(implied: to lift it)*
> *He had not been able to — he was too weak.*

> Sie **hatte** es nicht **gewollt**, aber sie stimmte zu.
> *She had not wanted it, but she agreed.*

💬 **Commentary:** This form is relatively rare — modal verbs without a companion verb mostly appear when the meaning is clear from context. In practice, you'll see the double-infinitive construction (*hatte … tun wollen*) far more often. But knowing both forms prevents confusion when you encounter the standalone modal Partizip II.

## 🔴 WORD ORDER IN PLUSQUAMPERFEKT

---

### Card W-1 — Word Order in Main Clauses

**Rule:** In a **main clause**, the auxiliary (*hatte/war*) occupies **Position 2**, and the Partizip II goes to the **end**.

```
Position 1    | Position 2 | Middle field        | End
Ich           | hatte      | das Buch schon      | gelesen.
Gestern       | hatte      | ich das Buch        | gelesen.
Das Buch      | hatte      | ich gestern schon   | gelesen.
```

**Examples:**
> **Ich hatte** das Buch schon **gelesen**.
> **Gestern hatte** ich das Buch **gelesen**.
> **Das Buch hatte** ich gestern **gelesen**.

💬 **Commentary:** The verb-second rule of German means that *hatte/war* always sits in position 2 no matter what comes first. What varies is position 1 (subject, time expression, object, adverb), which determines the emphasis of the sentence. The Partizip II is anchored at the very end — nothing moves it.

---

### Card W-2 — Word Order in Subordinate Clauses

**Rule:** In a **subordinate clause** (introduced by *nachdem, als, weil, da, bevor, sobald*, etc.), the **auxiliary goes to the very end**, after the Partizip II.

```
Hauptsatz (main)       | Konjunktion | Subject | Middle field      | Partizip II | Auxiliary
Er war müde,           | weil        | er      | die ganze Nacht   | gearbeitet  | hatte.
```

**Examples:**
> Sie rief mich an, weil sie das Paket nicht **erhalten hatte**.
> *She called me because she had not received the package.*

> Nachdem er gegessen **hatte**, machte er ein Nickerchen.
> *After he had eaten, he took a nap.*

> Ich wusste, dass er schon **gegangen war**.
> *I knew that he had already left.*

💬 **Commentary:** In subordinate clauses, German always pushes the conjugated verb (here: *hatte* or *war*) to the absolute end. With Plusquamperfekt, this means the order is: **Partizip II → auxiliary**. "…gegessen hatte" and "…gegangen war" are the typical patterns. This verb-final order is one of the most characteristic features of German grammar.

## 🔁 COMMON MISTAKES & HOW TO AVOID THEM

---

### Card E-1 — Choosing *hatte* vs. *war*

**Mistake:** Using *hatte* for motion verbs or verbs of state change.

❌ *Ich hatte nach Hause gegangen.*
✅ *Ich **war** nach Hause gegangen.*

❌ *Er hatte eingeschlafen.*
✅ *Er **war** eingeschlafen.*

💬 **Commentary:** The *haben/sein* choice is the same as in the Perfekt — if you know which auxiliary a verb takes in the Perfekt, use the same one in the Plusquamperfekt. When uncertain, ask: motion? state change? If yes → *sein*.

---

### Card E-2 — Forgetting *ge-* (or Adding It Where It Doesn't Belong)

**Mistake:** Omitting *ge-* for regular verbs, or adding it to inseparable-prefix or *-ieren* verbs.

❌ *Ich hatte das Buch lest.*  → ✅ *Ich hatte das Buch **gelesen**.*
❌ *Er hatte **ge**vergessen.* → ✅ *Er hatte **vergessen**.*
❌ *Sie hatte **ge**studiert.* → ✅ *Sie hatte **studiert**.* *(studieren → studiert, no ge-)*

💬 **Commentary:** The three categories that skip *ge-* are: (1) inseparable-prefix verbs (*be-, ver-, ent-*…), (2) *-ieren* verbs, and (3) verbs already starting with an unstressed syllable. Separable verbs insert *ge-* in the middle. Knowing these three rules eliminates most Partizip II formation errors.

---

### Card E-3 — Wrong Word Order in Subordinate Clauses

**Mistake:** Placing *hatte/war* before the Partizip II in a subordinate clause.

❌ *Nachdem er **hatte gegessen**, schlief er ein.*
✅ *Nachdem er **gegessen hatte**, schlief er ein.*

❌ *Weil sie **war angekommen**, rief sie an.*
✅ *Weil sie **angekommen war**, rief sie an.*

💬 **Commentary:** In subordinate clauses, the rule is always: **Partizip II comes before the auxiliary**. The conjugated verb (the auxiliary) is the very last word in the clause. This is the opposite of English order (*had eaten* vs. *gegessen hatte*) and a very common source of errors.

---

### Card E-4 — Using Plusquamperfekt When Präteritum Is Enough

**Mistake:** Overusing Plusquamperfekt when the sequence of events is already clear from context or a time word.

❌ (awkward/incorrect) *Zuerst **hatte** er gefrühstückt, dann **hatte** er die Zeitung gelesen, danach **war** er spazieren **gegangen**.*

✅ *Zuerst **frühstückte** er, dann **las** er die Zeitung, danach **ging** er spazieren.*

💬 **Commentary:** When you use words like *zuerst … dann … danach* (first … then … after that), the sequence is already clear — there is no need for Plusquamperfekt. Using Plusquamperfekt throughout a sequence of actions sounds unnatural. Reserve it for situations where you explicitly need to show one event was complete *before* another past event began.

Complete each sentence with the correct Plusquamperfekt form.

2. Als wir am Bahnhof ankamen, \_\_\_ der Zug schon \_\_\_ (abfahren, *sein*-verb).
3. Sie rief mich an, weil sie das Dokument nicht \_\_\_ (finden).
4. Nachdem er drei Stunden \_\_\_ (arbeiten), machte er eine Pause.
5. Ich wusste nicht, dass du schon \_\_\_ (anrufen).
6. Er \_\_\_ den ganzen Tag nicht \_\_\_ (essen), als er endlich nach Hause kam.
7. Wir erkannten das Restaurant, weil wir früher schon einmal dort \_\_\_ (sein, *sein*-verb).
8. Kaum \_\_\_ sie \_\_\_ (einschlafen), klingelte ihr Wecker.

<details>
<summary>✅ Answers</summary>

1. Ich **hatte** das Fenster schon **zugemacht**, als der Regen anfing.
2. Als wir am Bahnhof ankamen, **war** der Zug schon **abgefahren**.
3. Sie rief mich an, weil sie das Dokument nicht **gefunden hatte**.
4. Nachdem er drei Stunden **gearbeitet hatte**, machte er eine Pause.
5. Ich wusste nicht, dass du schon **angerufen hattest**.
6. Er **hatte** den ganzen Tag nicht **gegessen**, als er endlich nach Hause kam.
7. Wir erkannten das Restaurant, weil wir früher schon einmal dort **gewesen waren**.
8. Kaum **war** sie **eingeschlafen**, klingelte ihr Wecker.

</details>
